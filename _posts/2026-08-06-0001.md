***theme & perimeter***

06 August 2026, Singapore

In every way sentimental, this blog post, the first of the blog posts, will be a letter to the future. 
In this letter, I'd like to simultaneously scope and sculpt the blog, while offering a glimpse into what I am thinking. 
To this effect, there are 2 things I will cover: 1. theme; 2. the perimeter. 

Theme:

Here, as a user of social media, I see this space as an opportunity for me to do what I cannot on social media. 

In part, that is defined by what it does do. Social media, as I understand it, is about sharing snippets of daily life that are in a certain category of interest. On Instagram, these are typically social. To closer friends, they can also be short thoughts or remarks. Snippets are shared, in every sense a snippet — curated, not representative, impressions. On the other end of the social media spectrum, LinkedIn, while I use it less (at this point), it is a platform made to compartmentalise virtue signalling of a different virtue. People use it to show their accolades, how they "think" about certain events, and to offer a curated version of themselves to the would-be employer. My close friends scoff at this behaviour, my generation uses it instrumentally, and my parents are wholeheartedly on board. 

Hence, this blog should be the intersection between what I would like to post, and what Instagram and LinkedIn is not. They will not be snippets: I'd like to write longer paragraphs, for my readers and viewers to have the patience to dwell on them. They will be authentic — a reflection of myself, my thoughts, as they are, not glamourised, and not weakened with citations of accolades. 

In concrete terms, I would like to write about my areas of general interest: ***architecture (buildings and theory), art, photography, running*** and ***a general balancing*** of all these things that make up my life. Motivation permitted, I would love to deep-dive into specific topics that I find fascinating: For example, how many of Singapore's public functions (ie. the Parliament) is housed in colonial-era buildings? And what exactly does that mean for the City-State and its attitude towards colonialism?
 
I am very excited. 

The Perimeter:

The Perimeter is the edge of things. The Perimeter of my blog is the edge of my blog, if we imagine it to take shape. (I suppose it would be The Surface if it were 3D.) 

Very simply put, this Perimeter is demarcated by fear. Fear of my personal life leaking. Fear of error and falsehood. Fear of consequences I cannot handle. Maybe, the fear of judgement. 

I think of a situation mentioned in a book I had read as a teenager. It was in a school booklist. Singapore, Incomplete by Cherian George. I cannot remember the details, however, at some point, the book details that, because of political reasons, he is denied tenure and loses his job working at a university, teaching journalism to undergraduates. 

Maybe that was a lesson that my school wanted us to learn. It is certainly one of few I carry with me today. Learn to tread carefully around political matters in Singapore. Learn to weigh if it is worth it to speak up and speak loudly about something. How much does it matter to you and the people around you? What are the possible consequences, and are you prepared to handle it?

Now, beginning to write this blog, I am wary. Wary enough to write about the Perimeter. Yet I can't help but consider the other side of the equation, a phrase that has re-surfaced in my mind recently: the Banality of Evil. Not to mean that I live in an evil regime that is committing atrocities on the level equal to the Nazis. Simply, that I would not like to be keeping my head down, keeping quiet and obedient unconditionally. I think that is not too much to ask. 

Eichmann left the human responsibility for independent decision-making to the state, and followed orders that lead to genocide. In that way, he demonstrated that evil could exist in the everyday. Just like how people might, under orders of their higher-ups analyse data and design algorithms that get people hooked on social media. Or just like how people working in government jobs might, under orders of their bosses, type out documents detailing the razing down of hectares of forest, in the threatening age of global warming. 

Certainly the power of the individual could be limited — what would the resistance of a single employee do? Capitalism (money) wins in the game constructed by Capitalism. 

However, I suppose, it is a matter of principle. 

To that effect, I will keep my own wits about me, and think critically about the content that I decide to write and post. My independent thought will be the bouncer that mediates the Perimeter of my blog. 

Sincerely,

Sketchmachine
