***red***

I saw this line on SGAG's Instagram page: "What happens when Singapore runs out of Red?", as the topic of their short film discussing the physical lack of red-coloured objects — red traffic lights, blood, sambal chilli etc. 

Around this same time, I saw on the news and on social media, the average Singaporean standing up for our forests and its wildlife when our Ministry of National Development announced it would clear a significant part of Maju forest for building public housing. In this, I saw our *Red-ness* — our passion, our care and our piping-hot blood. 

Looking at our flag of red, white, crescent moon and five stars, I asked myself: what if we lost our *Red*? What would be left of us then? 

***blur***

In the popular format of Kareem Rahma's SubwayTakes, my take is: "*Sharpness is overrated.*" 

Why do pictures need to be sharp? Apart from showing and capturing details well, why is our instinct to worship clarity? 

Recently, I've been extremely fond of the pancake lens I've attached to my Canon 800D body. It is made of a clear plastic lens attached to a 3D printed/injection mold plastic casing. This simple construction makes it unbelievably portable and versatile. Thus, it minimises the only point of friction I have with taking out my bulky DSLR — the fact that it is bulky. The pictures above are taken on the 800D, with the exact lens in question. I love them. 

I suspect, due to quality of the lens, the images get more blurry towards the edges. I think it adds character. Think about the smartphone camera where everything in view is in perfect and equal sharpness. Why does it need to be so... perfect?

Sharpness and blur act together to create a great image. An image that is completely sharp may lose its focus. An image that is unrecognisably blur is nothing but abstract colours blending into each other. 

I think many things exist in duality, and exist *because* of their duality. Red and white. Sharpness and blurriness.
