In every way sentimental, this blog post, the first of the blog posts, will be a letter to the future. In this letter, I'd like to simultaneously scope and sculpt the blog, while offering a glimpse into what I am thinking. To this effect, there are 2 things I will cover: 1. theme; 2. the perimeter. 

Theme:
Here, as a user of social media, I see this space as an opportunity for me to do what I cannot on social media. In part, that is defined by what it does do. Social media, as I understand it, is about sharing snippets of daily life that are in a certain category of interest. On Instagram, these are typically social. To closer friends, they can also be short thoughts or remarks. Snippets are shared, in every sense a snippet — curated, not representative, impressions. On the other end of the social media spectrum, LinkedIn, while I use it less (at this point), it is a platform made to compartmentalise virtue signalling of a different virtue. People use it to show their accolades, how they "think" about certain events, and to offer a curated version of themselves to the would-be employer. My close friends scoff at this behaviour, my generation uses it instrumentally, and my parents are wholeheartedly on board. 

Hence, this blog should be the intersection between what I would like to post, and what Instagram and LinkedIn is not. They will not be snippets: I'd like to write longer paragraphs, for my readers and viewers to have the patience to dwell on them. They will be authentic — a reflection of myself, my thoughts, as they are, not glamourised, and not riddled with citations of accolades. 

In concrete terms, I would like to write about my areas of general interest: architecture (buildings and theory), art, photography, running and a general balancing of all these things that make up my life. Motivation permitted, I would love to deep-dive into specific topics that I find fascinating: For example, how many of Singapore's public functions (ie. the Parliament) is housed in colonial-era buildings? And what exactly does that mean for the City-State and its attitude towards colonialism?
 
I am very excited.
